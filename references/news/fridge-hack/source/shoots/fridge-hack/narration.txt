Hackers turned up the heat in the freezers of about seven hundred French supermarkets. And France says Iran did it!
Overnight in October, some freezers hit fifty degrees Celsius. By morning: melted ice cream, spoiled meat, and more than a dozen fires.
Nobody set foot in a store. Someone got into the control panels online, muted the alarms, started a defrost, and never let it stop.
Why are freezers online? So technicians can watch them day and night.
One professor's verdict. Quote: a fridge doesn't look like a security risk, and that's exactly why attackers like it.
Danfoss, which makes many of those panels, points to outdated software.
Three weeks later, the same trick hit stores from Sweden to Belgium. Even an IKEA. No word on the meatballs.
Someone even went for freezers at France's blood service. Staff caught it in time.
Neither has been tied to the first attack.
For the first, France now names a culprit: a hacking unit of Iran's Revolutionary Guard.
Prosecutors cite its methods, traces of Farsi, and Iranian IP addresses. France says it's certain. How exactly? It won't say.
Iran denies it. And its reply is ice cold. Quote: a government that cannot even manage the temperature of its own refrigerators.
France wants arrest warrants and European sanctions.
The official advice? Take the freezers off the internet.
