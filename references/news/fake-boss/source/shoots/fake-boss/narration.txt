Your boss calls you on video: wire five million dollars, and tell no one. His voice. His face. It isn't him!
Prosecutors in New York say three men and their partners tried that on thousands of companies, using A.I., and took about a hundred and twenty-nine million dollars.
The indictment says it began with an email in the CEO's name.
One read, quote: we need to loop you into a deal we've been working on.
Then, the call: the CEO's voice, and sometimes his face, made by A.I.
Next came a lawyer: also fake, under a real attorney's name.
One defendant, prosecutors say, kept a handwritten script. Its title: Lawyer Call.
And it worked, the indictment says. A Missouri hospital system sent four point eight million dollars. A Florida pet food company, five point two million.
The money went to accounts in at least nine countries, then into crypto.
Two defendants have aliases in the indictment. Rocky Balboa. Superman Cash. Impossible Mission.
One allegedly boasted that at laundering money he was, quote: number one all over the world.
But it didn't always work. At one New York investment bank, an employee approved the wire. The back office stopped it.
All three were arrested this year. These are accusations: they are presumed innocent.
So what stops a fake boss: sharper employees, or a rule that nobody sends millions alone?
