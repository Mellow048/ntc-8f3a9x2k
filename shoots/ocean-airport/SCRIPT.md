# The Airport Built on the Ocean

## Narration

Japan needed an airport, so engineers built an island in the middle of the ocean. Then the island started sinking. This is Kansai International Airport, built in Osaka Bay and opened in nineteen ninety-four. There wasn't enough suitable space for a big airport near the city, so planners looked out over the water. Ships brought enormous amounts of rock and soil to create an artificial island, and a long bridge connected it to the mainland. But underneath all that new land was a thick layer of soft clay, and heavy things sitting on soft clay have a problem.

The weight squeezes water out of the clay, and the ground slowly settles. Engineers expected some sinking, but parts of the island settled faster than predicted. So they planned ways to keep the terminal level. Under its columns, workers can use hydraulic jacks to lift sections and insert steel plates. The seawalls have also been strengthened and raised to help protect the airport from the sea. The island is still carefully monitored, and the airport keeps welcoming planes. It's a whole airport built on land humans made, with engineers constantly watching the ground beneath its feet.
