# Pig War — script v3 (short)

**Target: about 90 seconds.** v2 (232 s) is archived in `SCRIPT.md` history and its shot plan in
`SHOT_PLAN_v2_232s.md`; this is the cut that ships. Same facts, same deadpan register — the
twelve-year occupation and the arbitration are kept, the detail is compressed.

---

**1.** One pig started a war between Britain and America. The pig lost. Nobody else got hurt.

**2.** San Juan Island sits between Vancouver Island and the mainland. Twenty miles long, seven
wide, and worth arguing about.

**3.** In 1846 the two countries agreed a border down the middle of the channel. There are two
channels. Nobody wrote down which one.

**4.** So for thirteen years both countries owned the island on paper, and neither would move.

**5.** Britain got there first, with sheep. In 1853 the Hudson's Bay Company landed thirteen hundred
sheep and called the place Belle Vue. Six years later there were four thousand, plus cattle,
horses, and forty hogs that went wherever they liked.

**6.** Americans came anyway. Twenty-five of them, miners who had missed the gold rush, cutting
claims out of the Company's best pasture.

**7.** Then, on the fifteenth of June, 1859, a black boar got into Lyman Cutlar's potato patch.
Cutlar had already asked Charles Griffin to keep his hogs off the land. This time he had had enough.

**8.** Griffin wanted a hundred dollars. Cutlar offered ten, and said there was a better chance of
lightning striking Griffin than of him getting a hundred dollars for that hog.

**9.** Britain threatened to arrest him. So the settlers asked their army to come. America sent
sixty-four men. Britain sent five warships and two thousand men. The two sides stood facing each
other, and were told not to fire first.

**10.** They didn't. For twelve years British and American soldiers shared the island: church,
whisky, tennis, a garden. In 1872 a German emperor drew the line, and the Americans kept the
island.

**11.** Nobody died in that war. Only the pig. The garden is still there.
