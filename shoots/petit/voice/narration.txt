A man walked on a wire between two skyscrapers, thirteen hundred and fifty feet up and the police were waiting below.

Philippe Petit is twenty-four, a French street performer, and he has just seen a magazine photo of the towers.

So he decides he has to walk between them.

For six years, he studies the buildings in disguise. Reporter. Construction worker. He even makes fake ID cards.

Then he and his friends smuggle a steel cable, a pole, and a bow and arrow up the freight elevator.

You see, the towers had only just opened, and security wasn't finished yet.

That night, they shoot a fishing line across with the bow, then pull the heavy cable over by hand.

At sunrise, Petit steps out with no harness, no net, nothing.

For forty-five minutes, he crosses eight times. He kneels. He lies down. He salutes.

Below, traffic stops. Thousands stare up. Police rush to the scene.

Luckily, they can't reach him up there. Unluckily, they can wait.

When he finally steps off, they arrest him on the roof.

But the city loved it, so the charges are dropped. Instead, he performs free in Central Park.

And the Port Authority gives him a lifetime pass to the deck.

The towers are gone now. But Petit is still walking.

He said, if he sees two towers, he has to walk.

He still has his pass. He still walks.
