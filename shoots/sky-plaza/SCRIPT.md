# The Skyscraper With a Hole in It

## Narration

This Hong Kong skyscraper has a giant open space underneath it. People can walk right under the building, and the offices are still hanging overhead. It's the HSBC headquarters, designed by architect Norman Foster and his team. Instead of filling the middle with a forest of columns, the building uses a distinctive steel structure with massive supports and trusses. Floors are suspended from the structure above, leaving large, flexible spaces inside. At street level, the raised building makes room for a sheltered public plaza. Look up from there, and the building seems to hover over your head.

Escalators carry visitors up through the underside into the banking hall. Above that is a huge atrium, and a system of mirrors helps bring daylight deep into the building. The design also considered feng shui, a traditional practice concerned with how places relate to their surroundings. The open ground level became more than an engineering trick: it gave the city a covered place to meet, rest, and pass through. So the strange gap under this skyscraper isn't missing construction. It's the part of the building that makes room for everyone else.
