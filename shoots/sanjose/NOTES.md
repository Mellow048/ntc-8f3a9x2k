## S2. Wikipedia, "Spanish galleon San José" (read 13 November 2026)

<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_galleon_San_Jos%C3%A9>.

- "*San José* was a 64-gun galleon of the Spanish Navy, commissioned by the Spanish Crown and built in the Basque
  Country of Spain. Launched in 1698, she served as a flagship of the Spanish treasure fleet during the War of the
  Spanish Succession and sank in battle off Barú Island, near Cartagena, Colombia, on 8 June 1708."
- "The sinking killed nearly all of the approximately 600 Spanish sailors and officers serving aboard under orders of
  the Spanish Crown, making the wreck both a significant underwater cultural heritage site and a war grave of the
  Spanish state. The ship's cargo of gold, silver and emeralds is estimated to be worth about US$17 billion as of
  2023."
- "On its final voyage, *San José* sailed as the flagship of a treasure fleet composed of three Spanish warships and 14
  merchant vessels sailing from Portobelo, Panama, to Cartagena, Colombia. The Spanish vessels carried gold, silver,
  emeralds and jewelry collected from the Viceroyalty of Peru, including silver and gold mined at Potosí in present-day
  Bolivia."
- "During the battle, the powder magazines of *San José* detonated, destroying and sinking the Spanish warship [...]
  Of the approximately 600 Spanish sailors and officers serving aboard under orders of the Spanish Crown, only 11
  survived."
- "Based on the speculation that, similar to its surviving Spanish sister ship, *San Joaquín,* it had 11 million pesos in
  coins on board at the time of its sinking, worth up to $17 billion as of 2023."
- "The Spanish government has additionally emphasised the significance of the wreck as a site where nearly 600 Spanish
  sailors lost their lives in the service of the Spanish Crown, giving the vessel both cultural and memorial
  importance to Spain as a war grave."
- "The indigenous Qhara Qhara people of Bolivia have also asserted a claim to the treasure aboard the Spanish galleon,
  arguing that the gold and silver were extracted through the forced labour of their ancestors at the Cerro Rico mines
  of Potosí. They have called upon Spain and UNESCO to declare the galleon's cargo as shared heritage."

## What the film words carefully

- **Whether she exploded.** Most accounts say her powder magazine went up; Live Science reports that researchers
  dispute whether she exploded at all. The narration says "blew up", which is the weight of the reporting, and the
  script's Result records the dispute.
- **The value.** Every figure is an estimate: there is no complete manifest. The film gives a range, "four billion
  dollars to twenty", rather than one number, and puts no value on screen without a source tab.
- **The looting allegation is an allegation.** It is attributed to "a Colombian heritage watchdog", and the state
  institute's denial is in the same line.
- **The salvage firm's find is its claim.** "Says it found her"; Colombia's answer in the next line.
- **One cup or two.** The AP wire says one porcelain cup; the ICANH inventory as reported by Live Science says two
  complete cups and fragments. The film says two, following the institute's own list.
