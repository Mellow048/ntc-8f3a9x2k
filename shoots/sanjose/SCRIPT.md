## The story in one line

A Spanish treasure galleon sank off Colombia in 1708 with about six hundred people and eleven million coins; Colombia
found her in 2015 and will not say where she is; in November 2025 her first objects came up — a cannon, three coins and
two cups — while four claims on her, from four countries and one salvage firm, are still being argued.

## Facts

- A 64-gun Spanish galleon, launched 1698, flagship of a treasure fleet; she sank on 8 June 1708 in Wager's Action off
  Barú Island, near Cartagena, with about 600 people aboard. Eleven survived.
- She was carrying roughly eleven million gold and silver coins, and emeralds, from Potosí by way of Portobelo.
- Colombia found the wreck in 2015, at about 600 metres — 2,000 feet. Her coordinates are classified as a state secret.
- On 19 November 2025 the first objects came up: a bronze cannon, three coins and porcelain cups. The cannon went into a
  refrigerated container, the coins stayed in salt water, on the advice of the institute that runs the recovery, ICANH.
- Estimates of her value run from $4 billion to $20 billion. There is no complete manifest, so every figure is a guess
  with a source behind it.
- Four claims are live. Colombia claims her as national heritage. Spain says she is a Spanish warship and a war grave.
  Bolivia and Peru argue provenance for the silver. The Qhara Qhara of Bolivia, whose ancestors were forced to mine
  Cerro Rico, filed a claim in 2018 and want the cargo treated as shared heritage.
- Sea Search Armada, formerly Glocca Mora, says it found her in 1982 and wants half of everything, some $10 billion.
  Colombia says the coordinates the firm gave in 1982 were ten kilometres off. A 1984 decree cut a finder's share from
  50 % to 5 %.
- A Colombian heritage watchdog has alleged the site was disturbed in 2016 and 2022 and that the coordinates leaked;
  the state institute denies it. The case is at the Permanent Court of Arbitration in The Hague.
- President Gustavo Petro wants her raised before his term ends.

## Not confirmed, and how the film handles it

- **Whether she exploded.** Most accounts have her powder magazine going up; Live Science reports researchers who
  dispute it. The narration says "blew up", the weight of the reporting; the second shot is a fire, not a hull breach.
- **The value.** A range, not a number: "from four billion dollars to twenty". The on-screen plate says "no complete
  manifest exists".
- **The looting allegation is an allegation.** Attributed to "a Colombian heritage watchdog", and the institute's
  denial is spoken in the same line, with a plate of its own.
- **The salvage firm's find is its claim.** "Says it found her"; Colombia's answer follows in the next line.
- **One cup or two.** AP's wire says one porcelain cup; the ICANH inventory, as reported by Live Science, says two
  complete cups and fragments. The film says two, and the plate credits the inventory. The narration's "two porcelain
  cups" is the institute's own list.
- **Who owns her.** The film does not say. The last line is the question, and it is left open on purpose.

## Rules for this film

- No invented quotation. Nobody speaks in this film; the only words are the narrator's and the text on screen.
- Every number on screen carries a source tab (`· AP`, `· ICANH`, `· LIVE SCIENCE`, `· DAILY GALAXY`).
- The people are cartoon figures. Spain's counsel, Colombia's minister, the institute's official, the president and the
  Qhara Qhara woman are types, not likenesses, except where a name is on screen — and then only as a name tag.
- 9:16, 1080 × 1920, 30 fps. Captions in the house style: two words at a time, uppercase, one line.

## Sound

`audio/build_mix.py`, all of it synthesized. E minor at 84 beats a minute, in four: no other film in the series is in E,
and none is this slow in four. A six-note motif — E, G, F sharp, E, D, E — is the ship, and it is played by different
things as the story turns: low brass in 1708, one bell on "Holy Grail", a sonar ping in the dark, a plucked arpeggio
for the first objects, a pizzicato joke for the crockery, a brass chorale for Spain, a pan flute and a bombo for the
mountain, a music box in G for the salvage firm, a kettledrum for the ten billion, a semitone tremolo for the watchdog,
strings for "science", four brass notes for Petro, a music box for "so far", the tuba at full weight for the men still
down there, and finally four notes on bells that stop on the second degree.

Effects: the sea under the whole first line, a broadside of four guns, the magazine going up, timber tearing, the hull
creaking over, water into the hold; down below, bubbles and two sonar pings; the robot's thrusters and a servo; the cold
air of the container, coins into salt water, a cup set down twice; flags, a step, wind on the altiplano; the claim on
paper; a stamp; shutters. Every sticker and plate in the picture has a thud or a pop on the word that brings it in.

Mix: −14.5 LUFS integrated, true peak −2.7 dBTP, music 6.0 LU under the voice while someone speaks, and 7.4 LU under
