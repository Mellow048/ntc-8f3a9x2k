## The story in one line

A man near Lyon hired a digger to cut a hole for a swimming pool and turned up five gold bars and a bag of coins worth
about seven hundred thousand euros; the state said it was not archaeology, no heir came forward, and the gold stayed
with him — minus eleven and a half per cent, if he sells.

## Facts

- In May 2025, in Neuville-sur-Saône, about ten kilometres north of Lyon, a homeowner digging the hole for a swimming
  pool with a mechanical digger turned up **five gold bars** and **many gold coins**, in plastic bags, in the garden.
- The town hall put the value at about **€700,000**.
- He reported it to the mairie, which passed it to the **DRAC**, the regional office of the culture ministry.
- The DRAC found **no archaeological value**: the gold had been melted **fifteen to twenty years ago**, by a company in
  the Lyon area. It was not ancient metal.
- The bars carry **serial numbers**. The gendarmes followed them and found **no link to any theft**: the metal had been
  bought legally and nobody had reported it missing.
- The authorities looked for the **previous owner of the house**, who had buried it. He was **dead**, and no heir came
  forward with a claim.
- In November 2025 the **Rhône prefecture** confirmed the find could not be classified as archaeological remains, and
  the gold went to **the finder**.
- French **Civil Code article 716**: a treasure is anything hidden or buried whose owner cannot be proved and which is
  found by chance; on your own land it belongs to the finder. If the state rules it is of archaeological interest, it
  can claim it.
- **Finding it is not taxable.** Selling it is: a flat **11.5 %** on precious metals, including 0.5 % CRDS. On
  €700,000 that is **€80,500**, leaving **€619,500**.
- The finder has refused every interview. The local press called him "the man who got rich digging a pool".

## Not confirmed, and how the film handles it

- **The number of coins.** French reports say "plusieurs dizaines" in one place and "une quarantaine" in another; the
  film says "many coins" in the voice and puts "41 GOLD COINS" on screen with the town hall's estimate, which is where
  the figure comes from. The bars (five) are solid across every source.
- **The value.** €700,000 is the town hall's estimate, not a valuation: the film says "about" and attributes it on
  screen.
- **Who buried it, and why.** Nobody knows. The film does not invent a reason; the closing question lists the four
  ordinary ones as questions, not answers.
- **The tax.** 11.5 % is the flat rate on resale of investment gold; it applies when he sells, not when he found it.
  The film says exactly that ("finding it costs nothing, selling it costs..."), and the screen says "if he sells".
- **The man's identity, face and name.** Never given: he has not spoken. He is drawn as the house's stock figure in a
  flat cap and sunglasses, and the film says so in `cast.ts`.
