## The story in one line

On Monday 21 August 1911, with the Louvre closed, Vincenzo Peruggia, a former Louvre glazier who had helped fit her
glass case, took the Mona Lisa off four hooks in the Salon Carré, stripped the frame and the glass in a service
stairwell, and walked out with the panel under his smock — past the plumber who opened the locked door for him. Nobody
raised the alarm until Tuesday. The Louvre shut for a week; a week later thousands queued to look at the empty wall.
The police arrested a poet and questioned a young Picasso. Two years later Peruggia tried to sell her to a dealer in
Florence, calling himself a patriot. He served seven months. She came back the most famous painting on earth.

## Score

Written by `audio/build_mix.py`; every note and every effect is synthesized there from the kit in `templates/audio`.
The film is a silent-film comedy with a sad ending, so it is scored as one.

- **The tune:** a waltz in D, 90 to the minute, felt piano, pizzicato bass, brushes, and a music box on the tune.
- **The theft (shots 1–5):** the waltz, jaunty and unhurried. It thins to the music box as the pane goes on her case.
- **The smocks, the lift, the stairs (shots 6–9):** the same three notes as a D minor ostinato, a wood block on the
  beat.
- **The locked door (shots 10–13):** the waltz stops for a held low D, a drone, a tick for each try, and the plumber's
  door.
- **The empty wall (shots 14–17):** a clock, and a piano falling a fourth, D, C, B flat, A. "She's gone."
- **The photographers, the shutter (18–19):** a low brass step down, the great doors.
- **The crowd (20–21):** the waltz again with the tune in the bass and nothing on top.
- **The police (22–23):** the ostinato in G minor, a detective's piano.
- **His room (24–26):** the ostinato thinned to a heartbeat, a low D every four beats.
- **Florence (27–29):** the waltz in A major on the music box, warm — he thinks he is a hero.
- **Napoleon (30):** four bars of a pompous march in B flat, which stops when the frame wobbles: he did not steal her.
- **Leonardo (31):** a fife over a lute, four bars of 1516.
- **The police at the door, the cell (32–33):** a tolled D, and a clang.
- **The return (34–36):** the waltz in D with strings under it, the music box on the tune.
- **Today (37):** the music box alone, slowing, and a held chord after the last word.

Mixing is the news films' half of the same script: the voice at -14.5 LUFS integrated, the music ducked 4 dB under
speech, a 1.2–5 kHz pocket cut under the voice, a ceiling that keeps accents off the words, a limiter, and 2.0 s of
tail after the last word. Measured in the mix: -14.5 LUFS integrated, true peak -2.7 dBTP, the limiter taking at most
3.2 dB, and the music **5.6 LU under the voice while someone speaks** (the user's ruling: 5–6 LU; 9–11 LU was rejected
as inaudible). Word by word, the voice stands a median 5.7 LU clear of music and effects, the tenth percentile 2.0 LU;
six of 226 words come within 0 dB and are lifted in `WEAK_WORDS`.

## Known small things

- **Frame 1951 is reported as a pop.** It is the dark cell: the film shimmers for two frames at a time there (the
  window's thin bars and glazing sample differently as the camera pushes), and one of those frames passes the check's
  three-times-the-local-median rule. The frames either side were looked at; nothing lands, nothing changes. Left as
  it is: the alternative is a re-render for a sparkle in a dark shot.
- **Shot 30 (Napoleon) and shot 33 (the cell) are the two dark shots** (mean luma 0.096 and 0.089); they are drawn dark
  on purpose, and the grade is the same as the rest of the film.
- **The queue at the Louvre and the crowd in the Salon Carré are drawn from four reusable citizens** (a hat, a
  bonnet, a bowler, a flat cap), so the same faces come round twice in a crowd. Only Peruggia is drawn as Peruggia.
- **The crowd at the bare wall is seven backs,** and the four hooks show above their heads, not between them; from the
  low angle the crowd covers the darker patch of wall where she hung.
- **The join between shots 13 and 14 is a cut in the sound and not in the picture:** the plumber opens the door, and
  one step later the same take is still running. The check's measure puts the change at that frame below the cut
  threshold, which is what a join is for.
- **The score's own tune is new for this film:** a waltz in D at 90 to the minute, with a D minor ostinato, a G minor
  version for the police, an A major music box for Florence, a four-bar march in B flat for Napoleon, a fife over a
  lute for 1516 and a toll for the cell. No melody is taken from any other short in the repository.
- **Not done:** nobody has watched the film in motion or listened to it. The mix was measured, not heard: the
  balance of the waltz against the voice, whether the tick on the door is funny, and whether the ending lands are
  arguments from numbers and from the cue sheet, not from listening.
