# The Coldest Town on Earth — SCRIPT

**Facts (sources):**
- Oymyakon, Sakha Republic (Yakutia), Russia: widely cited as the coldest permanently inhabited place on Earth; population ~450–560; set in a deep valley ~700 m above sea level, far from ocean moderation. — Wikipedia; travel/reportage compilations (Eskify; BestMarkets).
- Record low for an inhabited place: −67.7 °C on Feb 6, 1933 (Guinness World Records listing, "lowest temperature in a human habitation"; some accounts cite −71.7 °C. Use −67.7 °C / "about minus seventy".) — Guinness (via NamuWiki mirror of GWR entry); Medium/Bold records of Yakutia archive.
- Cars must run continuously or oil solidifies; engines may not restart (~day wait); no mains running water (pipes freeze/burst); buckets/ice blocks used; outhouses; school closes only below −55 °C; eyelashes freeze; washing impractical — clothes "drop-frozen" and swung to dry. — Eskify; BestMarkets; NamuWiki.
- Boiling water thrown into −40 °C air bursts into mist/droplets that freeze before landing → falls as snow. — widely documented visitor accounts (e.g., Eskify; BestMarkets).
- The name "Oymyakon" is from the Yakut language and is commonly translated as "water that does not freeze", from the hot spring there (Tamtor). — NamuWiki; regional histories.
- Summers brief; only June–August see average above-freezing lows; July highs can still be cool (~20 °C).

**Voice (steady tone):**

Cars run all night, because if the engine stops, the oil gives up. The pipes froze decades ago, so water arrives the old-fashioned way. Schools close only below minus fifty-five. Eyelashes freeze mid-blink. And the laundry? It drops, it freezes, it's dry. Probably.
> In 1933, somebody recorded minus sixty-seven point seven degrees — a Guinness record for living humans. Throw a cup of boiling water into that air and it lands as snow. The droplets burst into mist, and the mist freezes before it touches the ground.
> Luckily — summer exists. Unluckily — it tops out around minus five.
> And the name of the town? Oymyakon means "the water that does not freeze" — named after the hot spring that makes the whole place survivable.
