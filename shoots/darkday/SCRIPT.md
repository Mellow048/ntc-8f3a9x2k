# The Dark Day — SCRIPT

**Facts (sources):**
- May 19, 1780: extraordinary darkness over New England & eastern Canada; candles required from noon; resolved by the middle of the next night. — Wikipedia, "New England's Dark Day" (via Encyclopaedia/timeline accounts); Harvard's Samuel Williams measured onset ~10:30 a.m., peak obscurity ~12:45 p.m.
- Roosters crowed, woodcocks whistled, frogs peeped at 2 p.m. in Ipswich, MA; strong sooty smell; rainwater carried a film of burnt leaves/ash; ash & cinders ~6 in deep in parts of NH; moon deep red that night; days before, sun appeared red and sky yellow. — Wikipedia, "New England's Dark Day"; Connecticut House of Representatives journal; Ebenezer Parkham.
- Most likely cause: smoke from extensive forest fires (evidence: tree-ring fire scars, 1780 fire in present-day Algonquin Provincial Park, Ontario) + thick fog and cloud cover. Smoke travelled ~290 km in ~7.5 h (~40 km/h). — "Fire scars reveal source of New England's 1780 Dark Day" (journal study), academia.edu; Wikipedia.
- Many feared divine judgment; people settled debts/affairs (documented witness behaviour).
