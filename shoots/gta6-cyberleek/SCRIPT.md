### Could not be confirmed, and how the film handles it

- **Who Cyberleek is**, whether it is one person or several, and how the footage left Rockstar. The film shows an anonymous hooded figure with a leek emblem and no face.
- **Whether a complete playable build exists.** Only the leaker says so; the film says nobody has verified it.
- **How much money was made.** Estimates differ ($200,000 to $270,000) and Cyberleek says it took trading fees only. The film says "at least two hundred thousand dollars" and attributes it to outside estimates, followed by the leaker's own description.
- **A comeback.** Smaller sites reported a post titled "RETURN OF CL" on 21 Oct and a poll for a leak on 28 Oct ([Ginjfo](https://www.ginjfo.com/actualites/logiciels/jeux-video-logiciels/gta-6-cyberleek-annonce-son-retour-et-promet-de-nouvelles-revelations-20261024)). No major outlet confirmed it and no new footage was authenticated. Left out of the film.
- **A $40 "development build" download** advertised on 13 Oct on a site linked to the leaker: unverified, and it is not known who posted it. Files sold as the game have been linked to malware. Left out of the film.
- **The "dead man's switch"** the leaker claims (story spoilers released if they are unmasked): a claim only. Left out of the narration.
- **Take-Two's exact words on pre-orders** differ between the call ("far in excess of") and the written release ("far above"), so the narration paraphrases and does not quote.
- **What the subpoenas produced.** Part of the court record is sealed.
- **The coin's value between the two reported figures.** Only the peak (11 Oct) and the low (13 Oct) are reported. The chart in the film is linear from zero in both directions and says on the wall: "Peak and low as reported by IGN. The line between them is a sketch." Where the line starts on 10 Oct is part of that sketch.

### Rules for this film

- Cyberleek has not been identified or charged: an anonymous figure, never a face. The narration says "they" and "the leaker".
- Motive stays attributed: "they called it a protest". The money figure is "outside estimates say". The full-game claim is followed at once by "nobody has verified".
- Rockstar's statement, Take-Two's position on discs and the leaker's own words each get their own shot and a card with the exact wording.
- Nothing from the leaked footage is shown or described. The leaked clips included nudity and story spoilers; neither appears. The film does not say where leaks can be found, and never shows the coin's name or address as something to buy.
- Jokes are about the situation (a leaker named after a vegetable, a threat that asks for a disc and an apology), not about developers who had their work leaked.
- Written for someone who does not play the game: one line on what it is and why it matters.

## Voice

Arena TTS `voice-15` (en-US, masculine, conversational), the voice the user chose for the Nvidia film. One take, kept as `voice/raw-take.mp3` (95.2 s). `build_voice.py` makes `voice/voice.wav` (73.06 s: 49 pauses tightened, tone almost untouched, pace lifted 1.2×, −16.5 LUFS) and `voice/words.json`, and puts five beats of silence back: after both "Quote:", after "…a public apology.", after "Two days later?" and before the closing question. The picture, captions, graphics and sound read their timing from `words.json` (copy at `remotion-app/src/videos/gta6/words.json`).

Voice ids are local to the session that made them, so the files are the reference. To change the pace, re-run `build_voice.py` with a different `--target-seconds`, then `audio/build_mix.py`; nothing else needs editing. `build_voice.py` needs `pocketsphinx` for the word alignment.

## Sound

`audio/build_mix.py` writes `audio/mix.wav` (74.43 s, 48 kHz stereo, −14.5 LUFS, true peak −2.2 dBTP) and the cue sheet `audio/cues.md`, which starts with the measured levels. Everything but the voice is synthesized in that script with the kit in `templates/audio`; nothing is sampled. The build is deterministic: the same inputs give the same file.

- Music: an original night-drive synth groove written for this film, 104 bpm in D minor (synth bass, kick, claps, a sixteenth-note arpeggio, a pad), and a two-note motif that belongs to the leaker.
- Level: the music sits **5.5 LU under the voice while someone is speaking, measured after ducking** (the standing benchmark in `IMPORTANT.md`: 5–6 LU was accepted, 9–11 LU was rejected as inaudible). Above 300 Hz, which is all a phone speaker plays, it is 7.2 LU under.
- Two things keep the words clear at that level, both taken from the reference film: the music's 1.2–5 kHz band dips 5 dB while someone speaks, and while a word is being spoken no 200 ms of music may come closer to the voice than about 3 LU.
- It gets out of the way of quoted words. One chord rings under Cyberleek's "a donation, never an investment". Only a pad and a slow arpeggio play under Rockstar's statement. One soft chord rings under the demand, and a seven-note music box answers "most polite threat I've ever heard". From "Two days later?" to "For now." there is no groove at all: a falling line as the chart drops, then the leaker's two notes.
- Ending: the groove comes back at 99 bpm so that four bars end exactly in the pause before the question. A D minor 9 chord lands in that pause and rings under the question, and two notes (A, then D) close the tune while the leaker looks over the counter. The picture holds 1.55 s after the last word.
- Effects follow the picture: stamp thuds, pops for the cards, alarm beeps when the lid opens, a crowd, a car pass, counter ticks, typing, a boing for the museum leek, coin dings, a cash register, footsteps and a gavel at the courthouse, search pings, a knock on the crate, rising ticks and a falling whistle on the chart, a crash when the coin hits the floor, a power-down, a door, camera shutters at the line-up, two cheers. Each is set relative to the voice (`fx(..., lu)` in the script).

The sound was set with meters only (loudness, true peak, level of the music against the voice per moment). The agent that made it did not listen to it.

## Render

`.github/workflows/render-short.yml` splits the film into 20 frame ranges, renders them in parallel jobs, stitches them, adds the mix, runs `templates/scripts/short_qa.py` against `qa-shots.json` and commits the MP4 and `qa-report.md` to the branch. On this branch the workflow's defaults point at this film; pass every input anyway so the command still works after the defaults move on:

```sh
gh workflow run render-short.yml --ref <branch> -f composition=CyberleekShort \
  -f mix=shoots/gta6-cyberleek/audio/mix.wav -f shots=shoots/gta6-cyberleek/qa-shots.json \
  -f publish='shoots/gta6-cyberleek/final-{size}p.mp4' -f size=720 -f chunks=20
```

A full run takes 8 to 11 minutes. When only the sound changed, add `-f parts_from=<run id>`: the picture of that run is reused and the run takes about 2 minutes. Parts are kept for 3 days.
