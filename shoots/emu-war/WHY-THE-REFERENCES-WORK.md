# Why the approved references work (measured, then looked at)

Read this before touching `story.ts` again. Source: `references/approved/koepenick` (26 shots, 69.7 s),
`beer-flood` (14, 65.5 s), `taipei-101`, plus their captured contact sheets and story tables.

## The numbers

| film | shots | mean shot | median | shortest | longest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Köpenick | 26 | **2.68 s** | 2.57 s | 0.90 s | 6.10 s |
| Beer Flood | 14 | 4.68 s | 4.82 s | 2.03 s | 7.27 s |
| My first cut | 10 | 7.32 s | — | — | 11.10 s |

The approved film cuts **2.5× more often than my first cut**, and the mean camera jump between shots is
**3.9 m** — every cut is a new picture from a new distance, not a small step to the side.

## The five things that make it read as film instead of slides

1. **One picture per clause.** On the contact sheet the printed caption under each frame is literally the
   clause being spoken — "POLICE HAVE", "SHOPPING.", "WEARING THE", "IN THE", "THEY DO.", "SEAL OFF",
   "A WARRANT". The words and the image are edited as one thing. My cut ran 2.8 lines per picture.

2. **The set is dense enough to be a place.** Brick facades with twenty window frames, timber floors with
   visible board lines running to a vanishing point, tiled roofs, counters, sacks, lamps, crates, banners.
   Nothing in frame is an untextured block. My district has a shed, a windmill, a tank and a crate — at any
   distance it reads as empty.

3. **The world labels itself.** "TOWN HALL · 1906", "POLICE REGISTRY", "SECOND-HAND UNIFORM", "MAYOR'S
   OFFICE", "TOWN TREASURY", "RECEIPT". Signs carry the orientation so the narration never has to. My film
   has one board and one permit.

4. **Scale changes every shot.** Wide exterior → tight interior insert → medium group → wide street. The
   alternation is the pace. My ten shots all sit at the same medium-wide distance, which is why it feels
   stiff no matter what the camera does.

5. **Foreground on every shot.** A desk edge, a doorway, a column of soldiers in front of the lens, a
   floor running under the camera. The frame is always layered near/mid/far. My frames are one plane deep.

## What this changes in the Emu War rebuild

- **32 shots stays** (2.3 s mean — close to Köpenick's 2.7 s, and the hook needs to be faster than the body).
- **Set work is now the job, not the garnish.** The paddock needs: fence posts with wire in depth, a wheat
  crop with real density, the shed rebuilt with a verandah, posts, tools, sacks, a water tank, a windmill,
  a truck with a visible bed and wheels, a camp of tents and crates, the dam with reeds and mud. Plus
  period signage: "CAMPION · WESTERN AUSTRALIA", "MEREDITH'S CAMP", "CULL PERMIT · 1932", the newspaper masthead.
- **Add the missing scales.** Two extreme wides (the flock as a mass at the dam; the district from the
  track), four tight inserts (the bolt jamming, the stamp, the tally marks, the permit), the rest medium.
- **Foreground layer in every shot**: a fence post, wheat heads, the truck's tray, the shed post, the gun
  barrel — something within 2 m of the lens, out of focus, framing the action.
- **Camera jump 3–4 m between cuts** minimum, and vary the axis: my current cuts all look down the same
  north-south line.
- Captions stay 1–2 words and land on the word, per the standing spec.

## The failure mode to avoid

Do not fix this by moving the camera more. Köpenick's cameras are mostly small moves — the energy comes from
**cutting to a different picture**, not from a bigger move inside one. A long move on the same picture is
what my first cut did ten times.
