An anti earthquake building? Watch this. The floor goes one way. The building refuses to follow. Mostly. This is not magic concrete. The trick is hiding underneath San Francisco City Hall. But first, imagine your kitchen during an earthquake. The table jerks sideways. Your bowl of jelly gets the full ride. Now let the tray slide. The table still shakes, but much less movement reaches the jelly. That's the idea. After the nineteen eighty nine Loma Prieta earthquake damaged City Hall, engineers couldn't just throw the historic building away. So they changed what it stood on. Under the columns, they installed bearings made from layers of rubber and steel. Heavy building on top. Shaking ground below. The rubber flexes sideways between them, reducing the shaking passed upward. It doesn't cancel an earthquake. The building still moves, just differently. And that gap around the base? That's breathing room, so it can move without hitting the ground beside it. So the secret isn't making a building fight harder. It's giving the earthquake a little less to grab.
