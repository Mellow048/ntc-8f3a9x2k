## The story in one line

U.S. prosecutors say three men and their co-conspirators used A.I. to copy the voices, and sometimes the faces, of
CEOs and of real lawyers, and talked employees into wiring about $129 million for deals that did not exist.

## Could not be confirmed, and how the film handles it

- **Whether any of it is true.** Nothing has been tested in court. Every claim in the film is given as the
  prosecutors' or the indictment's ("prosecutors say", "according to the indictment", "allegedly"), a tab on screen
  says whose account is running for as long as it runs, and the film says in words that all three are presumed
  innocent.
- **What the defendants say.** No statement found from any of them or their lawyers as of 12 Nov (fact 21). The film
  claims no denial and no admission.
- **Which companies and which law firms.** The indictment names none. The film invents no names: a hospital, a pet
  food company and an investment bank are shown as that and nothing more.
- **Whether a face was faked in any of the listed cases.** The indictment says the voice "and at times the image".
  Narration: "sometimes his face".
- **Which tools were used.** Not stated anywhere, and the film would not name them if it were (rule 17).
- **How often it failed.** Thousands of companies were targeted; the indictment describes one stopped wire. Narration:
  "it didn't always work", then that one case.
- **Whether any money came back.** Not stated. Left out.
- **Where the case stands after 5 November.** The release says extradition requests are pending. Shown with its date.
- **One figure differs between the two official documents** (fact 13, the mutual fund company). Not used.
- **"More than a year and a half"** is AP's summary of "at least" July 2024 to February 2026. Not in the narration;
  the dates are on screen.

## Rules for this film

- **The three defendants** are drawn as plain figures in different clothes, nobody's likeness: no photograph of any
  of them was looked for. Their names appear once, in the case title on screen, so that a viewer can look the case
  up. Two aliases belong to one man: the tags say so.
- **Hazan's 2008 conviction** (in the release) is left out. It is not needed to tell this story.
- **The prosecutor** is a figure at a lectern with his office on the tag, not a caricature: no likeness is known.
- **The boss, the employee and the lawyer** are invented. No company, bank, hospital or law firm has a name or a logo.
- **The fake** is shown as a mask held in front of a figure: a picture of the idea, not of any software. No tool,
  service, app or screen that exists is shown or named. The e-signature service the indictment mentions is not named.
- **The press release says "super intelligence"** where the indictment says "artificial intelligence". The film says
  A.I., the indictment's word, and does not go into the difference.
- **The joke** is on the aliases, the script with a title, and a pet food company caught up in a fake merger. Not on
  the employees who were deceived.
- **The closing question judges nobody:** sharper employees, or a rule that nobody sends millions alone. The
  indictment itself contains both: employees who believed the call, and a back office that stopped a wire.
