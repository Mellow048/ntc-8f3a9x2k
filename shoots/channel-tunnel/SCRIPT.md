# The Tunnel Under the Sea

## Narration

You can take a train from England to France under the sea. But you're not riding through a glass tube with fish swimming above your head. You're deep underneath the seabed. The Channel Tunnel stretches about fifty kilometers between Britain and France, with roughly thirty-eight kilometers beneath the English Channel. It opened in nineteen ninety-four. To build it, huge tunnel boring machines chewed through a layer of chalk beneath the water. Teams dug from both sides, aiming toward each other underground. And here's the part most people don't realize: there isn't just one tunnel.

There are three. Two large tunnels carry trains in opposite directions, while a smaller service tunnel runs between them. That middle tunnel provides access for maintenance, fresh air, and an emergency route if something goes wrong. Cross passages connect it to the train tunnels. And if you bring your car, you don't drive it all the way across yourself. You drive onto a special shuttle train, and the train carries your car through. So while ships sail across the water above, passengers and cars can travel through solid ground far below the waves.
