### Could not be confirmed, and how the film handles it

- **Who did it.** The FBI says North Korea. North Korea denies it. The U.S. president said "we don't know if it's North Korea". Three private firms agree with the FBI. Nobody has been charged, and the evidence has not been published. The film never states it as a fact: "The F.B.I. says", "North Korea calls that", each in its own shot with its own words.
- **How they got in** is Binance's own account of its own breach, published as an "initial report" while outside firms review it. The film says "Binance says" once, at the start of that account, and keeps a source line on screen through it.
- **The search ad.** Binance's report says only that the engineer found the rigged tool "after searching for it on a search engine". That it sat in an advertisement comes from one unnamed person quoted by Reuters and from company executives quoted elsewhere. On screen with "REPORTEDLY"; not in the narration.
- **Names the report leaves out**: the job site (one outlet says LinkedIn, citing executives), the chat app, the AI coding tool, the made-up company, the engineers. None is named in the film.
- **Whether the second engineer was looking for a job.** Reuters says he was "seeking a new job"; Binance says he had a profile on a job site and was contacted. The film says he "got a message on a job site".
- **What happened to the engineers.** Not reported. Binance says it has blamed no individual (row 18). The film says nothing about their fate and shows the company's line on screen.
- **Whether the faces on the calls were real people or deepfakes.** Not known. The film shows paper masks, which is a picture of "fake", not a claim about how it was done. Rooms, devices and the look of every screen are staged; none was reported.
- **How the internal AI tool was misused.** Not published. The film says "Binance's own A.I. tool" and shows a made-up assistant, no method.
- **"Most of the money in about twenty minutes"** is TRM Labs' reading of the blockchain, repeated by AP, Reuters and others. Binance's own timeline gives the first transfers (20:05–20:09 UTC) and the last (23:56 UTC). Both are on screen with their source.
- **Whether it is the largest theft of anything, ever.** One analyst's judgment; Reuters says "could be". The film says only "the biggest crypto heist on record".
- **How much has been laundered.** Estimates run from about $333 million to $1.28 billion "moved", by different definitions. Left out. The film says "less than half the money is frozen", which every count supports.
- **A viral story of a "honey trap"** at Binance: co-CEO Yi He called it untrue. Left out.
- **The finance chief's departure** on 7 November: Binance says it was planned six months ago and has nothing to do with the theft. Left out.
- **What the money is for.** Western agencies say such thefts pay for North Korea's weapons. Not in the film.

### Rules for this film

- Who did it is always somebody's statement. The FBI, North Korea and the president each get a shot and their exact words.
- The engineers are private people whom Binance has not named: anonymous figures, not likenesses of anybody. No joke is at their expense. The one joke is on the company that did not exist.
- The hackers are nobody: a hood with no face in it. Their "H.R. rep" and "CEO" are the same hoods behind paper masks.
- Kim Yo Jong and Richard Teng are named as the sources of their words and are not drawn. The president is the simple caricature of the first news film, with a name tag.
- Nothing that helps anyone repeat it: no tool, site or app is named, no wallet address or line of the malicious script is shown, every screen is made up.
- A number on screen is a reported number with its source beside it. The chart is drawn to scale from zero.
- Nothing here is advice about money.
- Written for a viewer who has never heard of the story.

## Narration (235 words, 73.9 s as read)

1. Hackers stole six point three billion dollars from Binance, the world's biggest crypto exchange. Their way in? A fake job interview!
2. It's the biggest crypto heist on record: over four times the last one.
3. Binance says it began in June, when one of its engineers downloaded a rigged copy of a developer tool.
4. Security caught it. But by then, the hackers knew who worked on the wallets, where the crypto sits.
5. In September, a second engineer got a message on a job site, from a so-called project director.
6. Interview one, on video: a fake H.R. rep, who shared personal photos to build trust.
7. Interview two: the CEO. Of a company that doesn't exist.
8. It ended with an assignment to download. It went onto a work computer. It was malware. Spoiler: there was no job.
9. Through that computer, and Binance's own A.I. tool, the hackers took over two wallet engineers' machines.
10. October twenty-ninth: most of the money was gone in about twenty minutes. The last, nearly four hours later.
11. The F.B.I. says North Korea did it. North Korea calls that, quote: an absurd rhetoric.
12. And President Trump? Quote: we don't know if it's North Korea.
13. No one's been charged, and less than half the money is frozen.
14. Binance says it's covering every loss, and that this wasn't one mistake, but a months-long campaign.
15. So whose job was it to stop this: the engineer's, or the company's?

**Cuts.** Two shots of the same thing from almost the same place make the picture hop at the cut (the lesson of the freezer film). Here every change of shot is one of three things:

- a cut to another set, or to the same set at a clearly different size or from another side (39);
- one take that holds two entries of the shot list, which switch the staging while the camera move runs on (`join` in `story.ts`): the chart, the first call, the closing question;
- a take that goes on from exactly where the camera came to rest (`soft`): out of the second call into the back room, and along the red string on the wall of months.

Sixteen changes of shot stay on the same set: the five that are not cuts, and eleven cuts that change the size clearly (wide to close, or back). The frames either side of each were looked at on stills, before the first render and again in the final picture: no hop. The joins as measured in the finished file are under Result.

## Voice

Arena TTS `voice-15` (en-US, masculine, conversational), the voice the user chose for the first news film and the voice of all three since. One take, kept as `voice/raw-take.mp3` (97.1 s). `build_voice.py` makes `voice/voice.wav` (73.92 s: 52 pauses tightened, tone almost untouched, pace lifted 1.2×, −16.5 LUFS) and `voice/words.json`. It puts seven beats of silence back (after "the CEO.", after "It was malware.", after both "quote:", after both quotations and before the closing question) and shortens the pauses the voice leaves between the letters of "F.B.I." (1.51 s as read, 1.32 s now; the first news film's was 1.29 s). The picture, captions, graphics and sound read their timing from `words.json`.

Voice ids are local to the session that made them, so the files are the reference. `build_voice.py` needs `pocketsphinx` for the word alignment.

## Sound

`audio/build_mix.py` writes `audio/mix.wav` (75.30 s, 48 kHz stereo, −14.5 LUFS, true peak −2.3 dBTP) and the cue sheet `audio/cues.md` (143 cues), which starts with the measured levels. Everything but the voice is synthesized in that script with the kit in `templates/audio`; nothing is sampled. The build is deterministic: run again on 12 November in a rebuilt environment, it wrote the committed file byte for byte.

- Music, written for this film: a caper in C minor at 130 bpm, swung (a walking plucked bass, a ride cymbal, finger snaps on two and four, damped vibraphone chords, a muted-brass riff that climbs C, E flat, F, F sharp, G), and a tune of its own for the fake company: hold music, a bossa in E flat major on vibraphone and glockenspiel.
- What the music does with the story:
  - The band stops on the cut to the video call, for "Their way in?", and four notes of the hold music answer under "fake job interview".
  - Bass and ride only in the office and at the engineer's home; snaps and chords in the hackers' room.
  - The hold music plays under both interviews, on the engineer's side of the call as well, and is cut dead when the painted office hits the floor, on "exist".
  - One held chord and two bass notes a bar while the file comes down; timpani and low brass a tritone apart on "malware".
  - Double time with a shaker for the twenty minutes, then one held chord while the clock's hands run to 23:56. It ends on the chime.
  - No tune under the three statements about who did it. A hit on "F.B.I." and a snare roll that lands on "North Korea". One timpani note, then North Korea's reply over one thin, high chord. Two bass notes for "And President Trump?", his sentence over one thin open fifth, and two plucked notes a tritone apart after it.
  - The band stops on the dominant as "campaign" ends. An open chord hangs under the question, and the film ends on the first four notes of the riff, which stop on the F sharp.
- Level: the music sits **5.7 LU under the voice while someone is speaking, measured after ducking** (the standing benchmark in `IMPORTANT.md`: 5–6 LU was accepted, 9–11 LU was rejected as inaudible); 6.2 LU under above 300 Hz, which is all a phone speaker plays.
- The mixing half of the script is the one the other news films use. Three things keep the words clear at that level: the music's 1.2–5 kHz band dips 5.5 dB under speech; no 200 ms of music may come closer to the voice than set ceilings, one for the speech band and one for the whole spectrum; and accents sit on cuts and gaps.
- Measured word by word, the voice against music and effects over each word's own span: median 5.8 LU, tenth percentile 2.4, lowest −3.4 ("the", in "half the money"), 7 of 235 words under 0. The approved freezer film reads 5.6, 1.5, −3.4 and 7 of 235 by the same measure. The measure is new with this film: `templates/scripts/word_margins.py <build_mix.py>` runs it on any of the news films, and this film's build prints it into the cue sheet.
- One word needed help, and that measure found it after the third render: "it", in "Security caught it." The read lets the word trail off 12 LU under its own level, and a beat of the bass landed on it: music and effects were 7.9 LU louder than the word. Now the word is lifted 5 dB and music and effects dip 4 dB for that word only (`WEAK_WORDS` in the script), and it reads 1.1 LU over them. The mix is the same outside 15.42–15.76 s: the largest difference anywhere else is −90 dBFS, which is rounding.
- Compared with the other three news films: they are in A minor at 112 bpm, D minor at 104 bpm, and G major with E minor at 118 bpm. This one shares neither key nor tempo with any of them, and none of them has a ride cymbal, finger snaps or a bossa. It shares a plucked bass and a vibraphone with the Nvidia and freezer scores, and timpani with brass on the big words with the Nvidia score.
- 37 of the 39 cuts carry a soft swish. The two cuts in to a quote (to the newsreader and to the president, closer) have none, and neither do the five joins.
- Effects follow the picture: coin streams, the counter's ticks, call tones, stamp thuds and pops for the graphics, pings as the old record is stacked up, typing, the click on DOWNLOAD, a two-tone alarm, a marker's squeak for each red ring, a message ding, the call's frame flying apart, a creak and a hit as the painted office falls, the download bar's ticks, a falling whistle, a buzzer, a hum along the wire, zaps, clock ticks and a chime, the last coin's clink, camera shutters, a gavel, ice, a cash register, a pluck as the red string reaches each month, a clunk for each spotlight. Each is set relative to the voice (`fx(..., lu)` in the script).

The sound was set with meters only. The agent that made it did not listen to it.

Three traps, fixed here and worth knowing:

- **The encoder can add a click.** In the first render the film's AAC track had five samples near full scale at 19.19 s, in a passage that sits around −17 dBFS in the WAV: a peak of +1.4 dBFS that no meter on the mix could have shown. The cause was not found, and it did not happen again outside that run (two versions of ffmpeg, nine encoder settings each). The workflow now decodes every encode and compares it with its WAV (`templates/scripts/aac_check.py`), tries other encoder settings if the comparison fails, and refuses a film that peaks above −1 dBFS. Every render since has passed with the first setting.
- **A quote read over nothing at all is a hole.** With no sound under the president's sentence, the check reported 0.4 s of silence in a pause of his (59.47–59.87 s). There is one thin chord under each quote now.
- **A jump in the picture that is meant needs a sound whose name the check knows** (`GRAPHIC_SOUNDS` in the script). The file dropping into the laptop and the president's shrug were reported as unplanned jumps until the whistle and the camera click were listed there.
