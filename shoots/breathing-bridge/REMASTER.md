# Bridge remaster

The remaster keeps the approved narrator, original mix, and one- or two-word timed subtitles. `remotion-app/src/videos/bridge-remaster/BridgeWorld.ts` contains the 19-shot design, including close views of thermal movement, interlocking fingers and a flexible seal, the workshop ruler analogy, a bridge bearing, and bridge traffic.

The bridge spans the frame and is supported by a pier, with paired towers, suspension cables and hangers, guardrails, sidewalks, roadway markings and lighting. A textured waterfront, city and water provide context. The road vehicles are externally sourced models; authors, source links, licensing and modifications are recorded in `ASSET-CREDITS.md`. The set, lighting, motion, textures and shot design are procedural and frame-deterministic. The selected shots use the same depth-of-field and ink/grade stack as the Taipei film.

`shoots/build_series_qa.py` reads the bridge shot starts from the shot list when regenerating `qa-shots.json`. Final output is 720 × 1280 at 30 fps with the existing 48 kHz mix. Previous versions remain in the branch's earlier commits.

The retention revision opens on the whole bridge before revealing the gap; shorter, narration-anchored cuts and purposeful dolly moves replace long holds. `motion.ts` contains the four exact warm/cold displacement windows: warm moves the left span right and the right span left (gap narrows), cold reverses those motions (gap opens), with no idle oscillation. Physical displacements are deliberately exaggerated to be visible. Direction arrows appear only while the spans move. `test-motion.mjs` checks direction, held endpoints and hot/cold continuity. The optional `audio/mix-hook.wav` adds a very low opening rhythmic layer to the original mix and is the mix used for this revision.

Final retention export: 31 shots, 720×1280 at 30 fps, 1852 frames / 61.733 s. Approved narration and 1–2-word captions retained. Finished-file checks: −15.0 LUFS, −1.3 dBFS peak, no silence, black frames, unplanned white frames, ≥12-frame freezes or unplanned frame-difference spikes. Representative final frames were visually reviewed, including warm/cold starting states, narrowed/opened endpoints, ruler heating and bearing sliding. These technical checks do not measure audience retention.
