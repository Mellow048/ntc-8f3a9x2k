# The Bridge That Breathes

## Narration

This bridge has a gap in the road on purpose. And without it, a hot day could make the whole structure fight itself. Bridges look perfectly still, but their steel and concrete get a little longer when they warm up, and a little shorter when they cool down. On a long bridge, those tiny changes add up. Imagine a metal ruler wedged tightly between two walls. Heat it up, and it has nowhere to grow. The ruler pushes against the walls. Now imagine that ruler is a bridge carrying thousands of cars.

Engineers give the bridge room to move with expansion joints: carefully designed gaps or flexible connections between sections. On a hot afternoon, the gap gets narrower as the bridge expands. On a cold night, it opens up again as the bridge contracts. Some joints have interlocking metal fingers; others use flexible seals. Bearings underneath can let the deck slide too. Cars roll across while the bridge quietly changes length. So that little clunk you feel at the end of a bridge? It might be the sound of a very big structure making room to breathe.
