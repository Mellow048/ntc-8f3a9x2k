## The story in one line

In July 2022 thieves took 24 bags of jewelry from a Brink's big rig at a California truck stop in the 27 minutes one
driver spent eating while the other slept; four years later one of the seven men charged has signed a plea deal, to
a count about a different truck, and nobody agrees what the jewels were worth.

## Could not be confirmed, and how the film handles it

- **What the plea agreement and the indictment say, word for word.** Neither was read. The film quotes only the
  newspapers' and the press release's own words, in quotation marks with the source; the page it draws says so
  ("as summarised by the L.A. Times and City News Service").
- **That the count he will plead to is the robbery of the other truck.** City News Service says it in so many words
  (fact 16). It fits the press release: the five men charged with robbery are the five accused of robbing the box
  truck's driver (facts 12, 13), and the jewelry heist is charged as a theft. The L.A. Times and Reuters name the
  count and do not say which event it is.
- **That he will plead guilty on 19 November.** It is a hearing that has been set. Narration: "agreed to plead
  guilty"; on screen: NOT ENTERED YET.
- **The burner number given to a police officer.** One newspaper, citing "two people involved with the case"; in no
  court filing that the reporting quotes. Narration: "the L.A. Times reports"; a tab says so for as long as that
  account runs, and the page of the newspaper carries the sourcing in its own words.
- **Which of the seven that was.** The Times does not say. The figure has no number, and a line on screen says so.
- **How the trailer was opened.** "The court filing does not say." The film shows a cut lock on the ground, which is
  what the driver found, and nothing else.
- **What the jewelry was worth.** Disputed in two lawsuits and in the plea agreement itself. Each number is given
  with whose it is, and the film ends on that question.
- **How much has been recovered.** "Only a small portion" (October 2025), "most ... still unrecovered" (January
  2026). Nothing newer was found. The date is on screen.
- **Whether the drivers did anything wrong.** The jewelers and Brink's dispute it in court. The film says where each
  driver was and nothing more.
- **Why a defendant was deported before trial.** Prosecutors say they did not know; the immigration agency's account
  of his record is left out. The film says what happened and what prosecutors say.

## Rules for this film

- **The seven men** are not drawn: a blank head with a question mark, a number and a colour. Number 1 is the man
  with the plea deal. His name appears once, on a line beside the plea agreement, as the court papers and every
  report give it. The other six are never named, and the film says all seven are presumed innocent until proven
  guilty, in the prosecutors' own words.
- **The man who gave the police a number** has no number: the report does not say which of them he was.
- **The drivers** are invented and anonymous, in a work shirt with no company's name; a line on screen says so.
  Nobody laughs at them: one eats, one sleeps, as the reports say.
- **The jewelers** are invented, older (their lawyer says most of his clients are senior citizens), and no joke is
  made at their expense.
- **Police and the prosecutor** are invented. No likeness of anyone who works on the case.
- **The truck** is plain white with no name on it. The truck stop is no likeness of the real one; the place name is
  real.
- **The other truck's driver**, who was robbed, is not shown. The film says the count is a robbery "by force and
  threat of force", so that the tags do not read as a harmless joke.
- **Where the men come from** is said only where the facts need it: an arrest on the way to Ecuador, a deportation to
  Ecuador. No stereotype and no politics.
- **Nothing that helps anyone repeat it:** no tool, no method. The phone number on the officer's pad is invented
  (555-0142) and the pad says so. The shipping form is a drawing and says so.
- **The joke** is on the situation: 27 minutes, a burner number handed to the police, a plea about tags in the
  largest jewelry heist in the country.
- **The closing question judges nobody:** which number counts, what the jewelers lost or what they declared. Both are
  positions held in court: the jewelers and the prosecutors on one side, Brink's and the defendant on the other.

## Narration (228 words, 12 lines), and the facts behind each line

1. Prosecutors say a hundred million dollars in jewels vanished from a truck in twenty-seven minutes, while one driver ate and the other slept. Now one of the accused has a plea deal. *(1, 4, 5, 15)*
2. July twenty twenty-two. A Brink's big rig leaves a jewelry show near San Francisco with seventy-three bags. The indictment says a crew followed it for three hundred miles. *(2, 3)*
3. Just after two A.M. it pulls into a truck stop. One driver goes in to eat. The other is asleep in the bunk. *(4)*
4. Twenty-seven minutes later, the lock is cut and twenty-four bags are gone. *(5, 6)*
5. For nearly three years, nobody was charged. *(9)*
6. The break, the L.A. Times reports: phones that pinged cell towers along the route. And one of the crew had handed police his burner number the night before, as a witness to a crash. *(10, 11)*
7. Seven men were charged. One was stopped at an airport in Panama. *(12, 14)*
8. This month he agreed to plead guilty to robbing a different truck, one carrying AirTags. In the same deal, he admits the jewel heist. *(13, 15, 16, 17)*
9. Three others have pleaded not guilty. Two were never arrested. One was deported before trial; prosecutors say they didn't know. *(18, 19)*
10. Most of the jewels are still missing. The jewelers put them at a hundred million dollars. *(8, 20)*
11. Brink's says they declared eight point seven million. *(7)*
12. So which number counts: what the jewelers lost, or what they declared?

One plus three plus two plus one is seven: every one of the men charged is accounted for in lines 8 and 9.
