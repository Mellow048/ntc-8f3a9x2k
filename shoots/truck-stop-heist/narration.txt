Prosecutors say a hundred million dollars in jewels vanished from a truck in twenty-seven minutes, while one driver ate and the other slept. Now one of the accused has a plea deal.
July twenty twenty-two. A Brink's big rig leaves a jewelry show near San Francisco with seventy-three bags. The indictment says a crew followed it for three hundred miles.
Just after two A.M. it pulls into a truck stop. One driver goes in to eat. The other is asleep in the bunk.
Twenty-seven minutes later, the lock is cut and twenty-four bags are gone.
For nearly three years, nobody was charged.
The break, the L.A. Times reports: phones that pinged cell towers along the route. And one of the crew had handed police his burner number the night before, as a witness to a crash.
Seven men were charged. One was stopped at an airport in Panama.
This month he agreed to plead guilty to robbing a different truck, one carrying AirTags. In the same deal, he admits the jewel heist.
Three others have pleaded not guilty. Two were never arrested. One was deported before trial; prosecutors say they didn't know.
Most of the jewels are still missing. The jewelers put them at a hundred million dollars.
Brink's says they declared eight point seven million.
So which number counts: what the jewelers lost, or what they declared?
