## The story in one line

A cyberattack made New Mexico's only Level I trauma centre send ambulances elsewhere for two hours; the group that
claimed it then apologised, offered the hospital a free key and patients up to $2,500 each, said it would keep the
data, and two weeks later claimed an attack on another university.

## Could not be confirmed, and how the film handles it

- **Who the group is**, how many they are and where. Nobody has said. Three hoods with no faces are a picture of "a
  group" and of nothing else.
- **Whether the group has the data it lists.** The university has confirmed none of it. Narration: "claimed"; on
  screen: UNCONFIRMED.
- **Whether a key was ever handed over or used, and whether any patient was paid.** Unknown (fact 11). The film says
  "offered", and the university's line that it made no concessions is given in its own words.
- **Whose account of the outage is right.** The university says outages made it divert ambulances and that the
  event never reached its core systems; the group says the university switched its own machines off. Each is given
  as that side's words, and the group's own line is there too: "Only we & UNM know the truth."
- **What the voice at UNM said.** Only AP's summary of what staff heard. No quotation is used for it.
- **Whether the same people attacked Vanderbilt.** The group claimed it and a note in its name was found; the
  university has not confirmed who did it. Narration: "the group claimed".
- **The words of the recording at Vanderbilt.** From The Register's report of a video that students posted.
  Narration: "reportedly"; the source is on screen.
- **Whether anyone was harmed by the diversion.** Nothing reported. Not claimed.
- **How they got in.** Not known, and the film would not say (rule 17).
- **That nobody has been charged.** No report found on 12 November. Dated on screen.

## Rules for this film

- **The group** is three hooded figures with no faces, told apart by colour. No name of a person, no handle, no
  address. Their site is never shown: what they posted appears on plain cards marked as theirs. The channel they
  ask to be contacted through is cut from the quotation and never shown or named.
- **Whose words.** A tab on the left edge reads THE GROUP'S OWN WORDS while the group's posts and claims are on
  screen (lines 4 to 8 and line 10). It is off for what the university says and for what reporters heard.
- **The warning comes with the offer.** The offer of money never stands alone for long: the university's "do not
  respond" follows at once, in its own words, with where to report.
- **Staff, patients and paramedics** are invented and anonymous, and nobody laughs at them. The hospital is no
  likeness of the real building.
- **The university speaks through its notice**, with its name and the dates. No spokesperson is drawn.
- **Vanderbilt** is a campus building with its name on a sign, because the incident is its own confirmed statement.
  Its hospital is not shown: it was not affected.
- **Nothing that helps anyone repeat it:** no method, no tool, no site, no way to reach the group.
- **The joke** is on the tone: an apology "for the inconvenience" from people who keep what they took. Not on the
  hospital and not on anyone who needed it.
- **The closing question judges nobody:** a conscience, or marketing. Both readings are in the reporting: a site that
  has covered breaches for twenty years had never seen such an offer, and the same group went on to threaten
  another university two weeks later.
