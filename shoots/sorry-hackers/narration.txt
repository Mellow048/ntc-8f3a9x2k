A cyberattack sent ambulances away from New Mexico's only top-level trauma center. Then the hackers said sorry, and offered patients money!
October twenty-sixth, the University of New Mexico's health system. Staff say a voice came from their computers, claiming their data was stolen.
For two hours, ambulances went elsewhere. Outpatient surgeries stopped for the day. By the next afternoon, the university said, systems were back.
A new group calling itself B.Y.O.D. claimed the attack, and the personal data of every student and employee.
Then it apologized. Quote: we did not know that it was running machines related to assisting patients in real time.
It offered the hospital a free key, and any patient affected up to twenty-five hundred dollars, for the inconvenience.
Quote: we have morals and we have a heart, we are not villains.
One catch. Quote: we are still going to keep the data.
The university says it made no concessions, and warns: do not answer. An offer of money can be one more scam.
The group hit back: the university, it wrote, had a full panic attack and switched its own machines off.
Two weeks later the group claimed Vanderbilt University, where computers reportedly said, quote: failure to communicate will result in this estate being brought to the ground completely.
No one has been charged.
So what was that apology: a conscience, or marketing?
