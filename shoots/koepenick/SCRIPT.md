### Rules for this film

- No money figure in narration or on screen: three sources give three amounts.
- The bath belongs to "a police inspector", not "the police chief" (the mayor
  was the police chief).
- The warrant exchange is the only reported speech, and it is paraphrased from
  S2. No other invented dialogue presented as fact.
- "Prison warden's name" rests on S1 and S3. S6 reports it as an invented name;
  keep the wording, and change it if a better source contradicts it.
- Do not state the Kaiser's amusement or any quote from him as fact. S1 says
  the "that is what discipline means" line is unconfirmed. The pardon itself
  is documented.
- Leave out Voigt's "I only wanted a passport" motive (the court called it
  implausible) and everything invented by Zuckmayer's play.
- Nobody was hurt. Keep it playful: the joke is on blind obedience to a
  uniform. This is Imperial Germany in 1906: spiked helmets, dark-blue tunics,
  horse cabs, gaslight. No later German military imagery of any kind.
- Costume reference for the fake captain (police description, S8): infantry
  uniform, cap, greatcoat with captain's insignia of the 1st Foot Guards, long
  trousers, boots with spurs, white gloves, officer's sash, sabre. White
  moustache (S7).

## Narration (235 spoken words)

1. In 1906, the German army helped a shoemaker rob a town hall. They had no idea.
2. Wilhelm Voigt is fifty-seven, an ex-convict, and the police have just banned him from Berlin.
3. So he goes shopping.
4. Piece by piece, he buys a second-hand captain's uniform. It's not even correct. He's wearing the wrong hat.
5. Then he stops ten armed soldiers in the street, and orders them to follow him. They do.
6. You see, in the Kaiser's Germany, nobody questions an officer.
7. He takes his new army by train, and buys them all a beer.
8. In nearby Köpenick, they seal off the town hall. The police even hold back the crowd.
9. A police inspector asks the fake captain for time off, to take a bath. Granted.
10. The mayor demands a warrant. The shoemaker says his soldiers are his warrant.
11. Then he takes the town's cash, and signs the receipt with the name of his old prison warden.
12. He tells the soldiers to stand guard for half an hour, and vanishes.
13. Luckily, the police have no idea who he is. Unluckily, his old cellmate does.
14. Ten days later, he's arrested at breakfast. Four years in prison.
15. But all of Germany is laughing, so the Kaiser pardons him.
16. Soon he's on stage, re-enacting his own robbery.
17. And outside that town hall today? A bronze statue of the shoemaker. Still wearing the wrong hat.

Setup and payoff: "wearing the wrong hat" (line 4) returns as the last five
words. The hook's "shoemaker" returns in the last line.

The exact text sent to the voice is `voice/narration.txt` ("1906" is written
"nineteen oh six").
